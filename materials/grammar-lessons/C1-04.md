## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. ???????? ???? ? ??????? ???????????

Participle clauses provide a compact alternative to some finite adverbial and relative clauses. The participle form signals whether the understood subject performs or receives the action and whether the event is simultaneous with or earlier than the main-clause event. A preposition or conjunction can remain before the participle clause to clarify its relation.

The scope follows Advanced Grammar in Use Units 58?59: adverbial participle clauses, perfect participles, implied-subject control, preposition + -ing, and conjunction + participle. A full clause is preferable whenever the subject or time relation is unclear.

### 4.2. ???????????? ? ?????????????? ??????

**1. Present participle (-ing): active, simultaneous or explanatory**

- Walking through the archive, the researcher noticed a missing file.
- Knowing the limitations, the authors avoided broad claims.

The understood subject is normally the subject of the main clause. Depending on context, the clause may express time, reason or accompanying circumstance.

**2. Perfect participle: earlier event**

- Having checked the figures, the analyst submitted the report.
- Having lived abroad for several years, she understood the local conventions.

Having + V3 presents an event as earlier than the main-clause event. The subject normally performs the action.

**3. Passive participle clauses**

- Compared with the control group, the treatment group showed a larger improvement.
- The proposal, outlined in the previous section, is evaluated below.
- Being interviewed, she remained calm.

Use a past participle when the understood subject receives the action. Being + V3 can present a passive process; a bare V3 can reduce a passive relative/adverbial clause.

**4. Perfect passive participle**

- Having been reviewed by two independent panels, the proposal was approved.

Having been + V3 marks an earlier passive event. The proposal receives the reviewing.

**5. Preposition + -ing**

- By working carefully, the team reduced the error rate.
- On returning from the conference, she revised the draft.
- After spending so much money on the project, he couldn't afford a holiday.
- Without being told, I would never have guessed.
- In criticising the proposal, the reviewer identified a methodological weakness.

After a preposition use the -ing form. By commonly expresses method, on/after time, without absence, and in the activity or context. Preserve the meaning of the preposition.

**6. With + -ing / without + -ing**

- With Louise living in Spain, we don't see her often.
- Without having a similar meaning to the phrase, the example would be misleading.

With + -ing can introduce an accompanying circumstance; without + -ing expresses the absence of an action or condition. Ensure the understood subject is clear.

**7. Conjunction + participle**

- While waiting for the results, the team reviewed the protocol.
- Although criticised by several reviewers, the paper was accepted.
- If asked, provide the original measurements.
- When combined with the control data, the results show a different pattern.

With conjunctions such as while, when, although and if, a reduced clause is possible when the subject is understood and the reduced form remains clear. A finite clause is safer if the subjects differ or the reference is ambiguous.

**8. Subject control and dangling modifiers**

- Correct: Having checked the figures, the analyst submitted the report.
- Incorrect: Having checked the figures, the report was submitted. (The report did not check the figures.)
- Correct alternative: After the analyst had checked the figures, the report was submitted.

The understood subject normally matches the main-clause subject. Do not use a comma to disguise a mismatch.

### 4.3. ??? ??????? ?????????? ?????

1. Restore the full finite clause: who performs/receives the action, and when?
2. For an active simultaneous or accompanying event, consider -ing.
3. For an earlier active event, consider having + V3.
4. For a passive relation, use V3, being + V3 or having been + V3 according to time and aspect.
5. After a preposition, use -ing.
6. With while/when/although/if + participle, omit the subject only when it is recoverable and the reduced clause remains clear.
7. Check that the understood subject matches the main clause; otherwise use a full clause or a clearly licensed construction with its own subject.

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- The understood subject of an adverbial participle clause normally matches the subject of the main clause. Otherwise the modifier may dangle.
- An -ing clause does not always express time; context can show reason, method or accompanying circumstance.
- Having + V3 normally marks an earlier active event; do not use it automatically for simultaneous actions.
- Use having been + V3 for an earlier passive event. Use being + V3 for a passive process or a suitable reduced passive clause.
- After a preposition (by, on, after, in, without, with), use the -ing form when the construction requires a gerund-participial clause.
- With while/when/although/if + participle, omit the subject only when it is recoverable and the reduced clause is clear.
- A full finite clause is preferable when subjects differ, time sequence is uncertain or the sentence becomes difficult to process.
- Unit 58 includes some participle clauses with an explicitly expressed subject; do not assume every participle clause is subjectless.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `Having subjected to repeated testing, the material showed no structural change.` | `Having been subjected to repeated testing, the material showed no structural change.` | Having + V3 is active; the material receives testing. |
| `The evidence having reviewed, the committee approved the recommendation.` | `The evidence having been reviewed, the committee approved the recommendation.` | Having reviewed would mean the evidence performed the review. |
| `Recognised the limitations of the dataset, the authors avoided broad claims.` | `Recognising the limitations of the dataset, the authors avoided broad claims.` | Recognised would be passive and leave the logical subject unclear. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. ??????? ???????

- **-ing:** active simultaneous/accompanying action, reason or method, depending on context.
- **Having + V3:** earlier active event.
- **V3 / being + V3:** passive description or process; **having been + V3** = earlier passive event.
- **Preposition + -ing:** by/on/after/in/without/with + verb-ing.
- **Conjunction + participle:** while/when/although/if + participle when the subject is clear.
- Check the understood subject; use a full finite clause if the reference is ambiguous.
