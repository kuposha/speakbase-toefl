## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Cleft structures divide a sentence into a focused element and background information. They guide the reader/listener toward the information that answers a particular question or corrects an assumption.

Тема этого блока — **Cleft clauses and information focus**. Измеримая цель: Использовать it-clefts/what-clauses и другие способы вынести важную информацию

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Existential there**

- **There is/are + noun phrase** introduces existence/presence: There are several reasons for the delay.
- Choose is/was with a singular or uncountable head and are/were with a plural head. In complex phrases, identify the real noun head.
- There can be followed by a relative clause, infinitive or participle phrase: There is a report to finish; there were several people waiting.

**2. Introductory it**

Use **it** as a grammatical subject when the real content is a to-infinitive or that-clause:
- It is difficult to explain the result.
- It is possible that the data were corrupted.
- It took us two hours to finish the report.
- It seems that the plan has changed.

The it does not refer to a concrete object; the infinitive/that-clause carries the main content.

**3. It as anticipatory object**

Some verbs take **it + adjective/noun + to-infinitive/that-clause**:
- I find it difficult to concentrate.
- We thought it strange that nobody complained.
- She made it clear that the deadline would not change.

Here it is the object of the verb; the following clause explains what it refers to.

**4. It-cleft**

- **It + be + focused element + that/who-clause**: It was the final paragraph that caused confusion.
- Focus can be a person, thing, time, place or reason.
- Use **who** for a focused person when it is natural; **that** is also possible with people in many it-clefts and is common with things, times and other focus types.
- Keep the tense of be consistent with the original message.

**5. Wh-/what-cleft**

- **What + clause + be + focus**: What we need is a clearer explanation.
- **All (that) + clause + be + focus**: All I wanted was an answer.
- The what-clause is the topic; the complement after be carries the focus. Singular agreement is the usual default (*What we need is a larger sample*), though a plural complement can sometimes attract plural agreement in informal or notional usage.

**6. Choosing the structure**

Use there to introduce existence/presence, introductory it to present an evaluation or proposition, anticipatory object it after a verb, and a cleft to contrast or highlight one element. These structures are not interchangeable: each organises information differently.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. If the sentence introduces existence/presence, use there + be and make the verb agree with the real noun head.
2. If a to-infinitive or that-clause is the logical subject, consider introductory it.
3. After verbs such as find, think and make, check whether anticipatory object it is required before the adjective/noun complement.
4. If one element needs contrastive focus, choose an it-cleft.
5. If the whole action/proposition should be the topic and the key information the complement, choose a what-cleft.
6. Choose who/that and tense in the cleft clause according to the focused element and original time.
7. Check that the structure preserves the original meaning and does not create unnecessary emphasis.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

The second experiment revealed the pattern. → It was the second experiment that revealed the pattern. What the team needs is more time.

| Что проверяем | Вопрос для выбора |
|---|---|
| Форма | Какие элементы обязательны и в каком порядке они стоят? |
| Значение | Что именно меняется: время, длительность, реальность, степень уверенности или фокус? |
| Контекст | Что уже известно собеседнику и каков регистр высказывания? |
| Проверка | Если заменить форму близкой конструкцией, изменится ли смысл? Объясни, почему. |

### 4.5. Примеры с переводом и объяснением

| English | Перевод | Почему это подходит |
|---|---|---|---|
| `It was the final experiment that revealed the unexpected pattern.` | Именно последний эксперимент выявил неожиданную закономерность. | That introduces the cleft relative clause focused on the experiment. |
| `What the team needs is a larger sample.` | Команде нужна более крупная выборка. | What introduces the wh-cleft clause functioning as the subject. |
| `It was Maria who identified the error, not Daniel.` | Именно Мария обнаружила ошибку, а не Дэниел. | Who focuses a person in an it-cleft. |
| `What surprised the reviewers was the lack of supporting data.` | Именно отсутствие подтверждающих данных удивило рецензентов. | The wh-cleft subject clause is treated as singular here; use was. |
| `I found it difficult to concentrate.` | Мне трудно сосредоточиться. | It is the anticipatory object after find; the to-infinitive explains what is difficult. |
| `It is possible that the data were corrupted.` | Возможно, данные были испорчены. | Introductory it is the grammatical subject; the that-clause carries the proposition. |
| `There are several reasons for the delay.` | Есть несколько причин задержки. | Existential there introduces presence; are agrees with the plural head reasons. |
| `All I wanted was an answer.` | Всё, чего я хотел(а), — это ответ. | All-cleft presents the whole clause as the topic and the answer as the exhaustive focus. |

Примеры составлены Speakbase. Они иллюстрируют грамматическую функцию, но не подменяют индивидуальную проверку всех возможных вариантов и исключений.

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- There is/are introduces existence; it is not the same as introductory it.
- Agreement in there-constructions follows the real noun phrase, not the word there; complex lists can sound awkward, so rewrite when needed.
- Introductory it is a grammatical subject; it does not refer to a concrete object.
- Anticipatory object it is required by some verb + complement patterns (find it difficult, make it clear); not every verb uses it.
- It-clefts and what-clefts change information focus; they are not decorative alternatives.
- Use **who** for a focused person when it is natural; **that** is also possible with people in many it-clefts and is common with other focus types.
- Keep the tense of be consistent with the intended time; do not duplicate the focused element inside the cleft clause.
- All-clefts can imply exhaustiveness; use only when that is the intended meaning.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `It was the final experiment where revealed the unexpected pattern.` | `It was the final experiment that revealed the unexpected pattern.` | Where refers to place, not the focused subject experiment. |
| `It the team needs is a larger sample.` | `What the team needs is a larger sample.` | It cannot introduce this free relative clause without a complement structure. |
| `It was Maria which identified the error, not Daniel.` | `It was Maria who identified the error, not Daniel.` | Which is for things, not Maria. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.8. Произношение и восприятие на слух

In it-clefts, the stressed focus carries the contrast (*It was MARIA who found it*); in what-clefts, the focus commonly falls on the complement (*What we need is MORE TIME*). In connected speech, unstressed it/was/that are usually reduced; verify exact phonetic forms with dictionary audio.

Для аудирования распознавай слабые формы вспомогательных глаголов, окончания и границы смысловых групп. Не ориентируйся только на чтение по буквам.

### 4.10. Примеры для управляемого разбора

- It was the final experiment ___ revealed the unexpected pattern. => `that` (the focused noun is the experiment).
- ___ the team needs is a larger sample. => `What` (the free relative clause is the subject).
- It was Maria ___ identified the error, not Daniel. => `who` (the focus is a person).
- All the committee wanted ___ a clearer explanation. => `was` (all-cleft with a singular complement).
- I found ___ difficult to explain the unexpected result. => `it` (anticipatory object).
- ___ is possible that the original data were corrupted. => `It` (introductory subject).
- In formal standard English, there ___ several reasons for the delay. => `are` (the real noun head is plural).

Для каждого примера назови целевой элемент, объясни форму, укажи, почему отвлекающий вариант неверен, и придумай собственное предложение с тем же правилом.

### 4.11. Самопроверка

Перед отправкой ответа проверь:
1. Правильно ли определена функция конструкции?
2. Согласована ли форма с подлежащим и временной перспективой?
3. Не перенесён ли русский порядок слов напрямую?
4. Не смешаны ли две похожие модели?
5. Не сильнее ли утверждение, чем позволяют контекст и доказательства?

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- There + be = existence/presence; verb agrees with the real noun head.
- Introductory it + be + adjective/noun + to-infinitive/that-clause.
- Verb + it + adjective/noun + to-infinitive/that-clause = anticipatory object.
- It-cleft: It + be + focus + that/who-clause.
- What-cleft: What + clause + be + focus; all-cleft = All (that) + clause + be + focus.
- Use each structure for its information function: existence, evaluation, object complement or contrastive focus.
