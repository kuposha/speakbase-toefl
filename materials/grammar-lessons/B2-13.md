## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Adverb position affects scope and information focus. Focus adverbs such as only, even, just and particularly can change which element is restricted or emphasised; stance adverbs express the speaker's evaluation of a whole proposition.

Тема этого блока — **Adverb position, degree, focus and viewpoint**. Измеримая цель: Использовать stance/viewpoint/comment adverbs и управлять фокусом высказывания

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. -ing and -ed adjectives**

- **-ing** adjectives often describe what causes a feeling: *an interesting lecture, a tiring journey*.
- **-ed** adjectives often describe the experiencer's state: *interested students, tired passengers*.
- Learn the adjective with its complement: *interested in, bored with, disappointed by/with*.

**2. Adjective order and linking verbs**

Opinion adjectives normally precede fact adjectives: *a beautiful old wooden table*. When several fact adjectives occur, a common order is size ? age ? colour ? origin ? material. Linking verbs (be, seem, become, feel, look, sound, smell, taste) normally take an adjective as subject complement: *The soup tastes good*, not *tastes well*.

**3. Adjectives and adverbs**

Adjectives describe nouns or follow linking verbs; adverbs modify verbs, adjectives, other adverbs or participles:
- *a careful driver / drives carefully*;
- *a surprisingly easy test*;
- *seriously injured*.
Many adverbs use -ly, but not all. Some -ly words are adjectives (friendly, lovely, lonely).

**4. Good/well, fast/hard/late and hardly**

- **Good** is an adjective; **well** is usually the adverb (*plays well*) and can also mean 'in good health'.
- **Fast, hard and late** can be adjectives and adverbs.
- **Hardly** means 'almost not/very little', not 'with effort': *She hardly spoke; she works hard*.
- **Lately** means 'recently'; it is not the adverb form of late in the same meaning.

**5. So and such**

- **So + adjective/adverb**: *so tired, so quickly*.
- **Such + noun phrase**: *such a difficult task, such nice people*.
- Use **so/such ... that** for a result; that can often be omitted.
- **No such + noun** means that something of that kind does not exist.
- Do not use *a so difficult task*; use *such a difficult task*.

**6. Too and enough**

- **Too + adjective/adverb** = more than is necessary/acceptable: *too expensive, too slowly*.
- **Adjective/adverb + enough** = as much/as good as necessary: *old enough, quickly enough*.
- **Enough + noun**: *enough time, enough chairs*.
- **Too/enough + for + noun/person**; **too/enough + to-infinitive**: *too heavy to carry; wide enough for two cars to pass*.
- Do not add an object pronoun after an adjective + too/enough + to-infinitive when the object is already understood: *too hot to eat*, not *too hot to eat it*.

**7. Quite, pretty, rather and fairly**

- **Quite/pretty** often mean 'less than very, but more than a little'; pretty is informal.
- **Rather** is common with negative/unpleasant descriptions; with positive descriptions it can mean 'unusually/surprisingly'.
- **Fairly** is weaker than quite/rather/pretty.
- **Quite** can also mean 'completely' with adjectives such as sure, right, true, clear, different and impossible, and with some verbs (*quite agree*). Context determines the meaning.
- Before a/an, use **quite a/an + adjective + noun**: *quite an old house*.

**8. Comparatives and superlatives**

- Short adjectives/adverbs usually take -er/-est; longer forms usually use more/most; some two-syllable adjectives allow both. Learn irregular forms (good/well ? better/best; bad/badly ? worse/worst; far ? further/farther).
- Use **than** after a comparative; use **as + adjective/adverb + as** for equality and **not as/so ... as** for inequality.
- Intensify comparatives with much, a lot, far, a bit, a little or slightly; use **any/no + comparative** in questions/negative comparisons.
- Repeated comparatives show continuous change (*better and better; more and more difficult*).
- **The + comparative..., the + comparative...** expresses linked change.
- Use **the + superlative**; after superlatives, use **in** for places/groups and **of** for periods of time. **One of + superlative** takes a plural noun.

**9. Word order: verb, object, place and time**

The verb and its object normally stay together: *I like my job very much*, not *I like very much my job*. Place usually follows the verb/object; time normally follows place, though time can be placed initially for emphasis.

**10. Adverb position with the verb**

- With a one-word main verb, frequency/stance adverbs normally come before it: *She always drives*.
- With be, they normally come after be: *She is always late*.
- With a multi-word verb, they usually follow the first auxiliary: *She has always been kind; I can never remember*.
- Probably normally precedes a negative auxiliary: *I probably won't go*.
- All/both often appear in mid-position: *We all agreed; they have both applied*.
- Keep focus adverbs close to the word/phrase they modify.

**11. Still, any more, no longer, already and yet**

- **Still** = the situation continues; it usually appears in mid-position.
- **Not ... any more/any longer** = the situation has stopped; these expressions normally go at the end.
- **No longer** goes in mid-position.
- **Already** = sooner than expected; usually mid-position or end-position.
- **Yet** is common in negatives/questions and normally goes at the end; it signals that the speaker expects something to happen.
- **Still ... not** can express stronger surprise/impatience than not ... yet.


**12. Even as a focus adverb and even in concession**

- **Even** marks something surprising or unexpected: *Even the director disagreed; she has even been to Antarctica.*
- It can appear in mid-position, with negatives (*not even, can't even*) or before a comparative (*even better, even less*).
- **Even though/even when/even if + clause**: even though presents a fact; even when refers to a repeated/expected situation; even if presents a hypothetical condition.
- Do not use *even + subject + verb* as a substitute for even though/even if: use the full conjunction.
- **Even if** is not the same as **if**: even if says the result remains despite the condition; if makes the result depend on the condition.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Decide whether the word describes a noun/state (adjective) or modifies an action/quality/proposition (adverb).
2. For -ing/-ed adjectives, ask whether the subject causes the feeling or experiences it.
3. After linking verbs, use an adjective complement; after action verbs, choose an adverb when describing how the action is done.
4. Choose good/well, hard/hardly and late/lately by meaning, not by adding -ly mechanically.
5. Use so + adjective/adverb and such + noun phrase; use too/enough according to whether the degree is excessive or sufficient.
6. Identify the meaning of quite (degree vs completely) and choose fairly/rather/pretty according to strength and register.
7. For comparison, select -er/more, as...as or a superlative; check irregular forms and the correct preposition after a superlative.
8. Place intensifiers before comparatives; use repeated comparatives and the...the... for change/dependence.
9. Keep verb + object together; place place/time phrases in their natural order.
10. Position mid-position adverbs according to the verb phrase; put focus adverbs next to their target.
11. Choose still/any more/no longer/already/yet by whether the situation continues, has stopped, happened sooner than expected or is still awaited.\n12. Place even next to the surprising/focused element; choose even though (fact), even when (repeated situation) or even if (hypothetical condition).

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- -ing/-ed is a useful tendency, not a mechanical rule for every adjective; learn lexical patterns and complements.
- Adjective order is a strong tendency; not every combination has one rigid order. Opinion adjectives usually precede fact adjectives.
- Linking verbs take adjective complements; do not use an adverb simply because the verb is present.
- Not all adverbs end in -ly; some -ly words are adjectives. Hard/hardly and late/lately have different meanings.
- Good is an adjective; well is usually an adverb but can describe health.
- So modifies adjectives/adverbs; such introduces a noun phrase. Use such a, not a such.
- Enough follows adjectives/adverbs but precedes nouns; too/enough + to-infinitive can omit an understood object pronoun.
- Quite can mean 'moderately' or 'completely'; adjective choice and context decide. Pretty is informal; fairly is weaker.
- Some two-syllable adjectives allow both -er and more; irregular comparatives must be learned.
- Elder is mainly used before a family noun (my elder sister); older is the general comparative.
- Use in for places/groups and of for periods after superlatives; one of + superlative takes a plural noun.
- Verb and object normally stay together; do not insert an adverb between them.
- Adverb position is not free: be, one-word verbs and multi-word verb phrases have different mid-position patterns.
- Still, already and yet have different time/aspect meanings; do not substitute them mechanically.
- Focus adverb placement changes meaning (Only Maria..., Maria only..., Maria reviewed only...).

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `Even the final chapter discusses the limitations; the earlier chapters do not.` | `Only the final chapter discusses the limitations; the earlier chapters do not.` | Even adds unexpected emphasis, not the intended restriction. |
| `The findings were bare statistically significant.` | `The findings were barely statistically significant.` | Bare is an adjective and cannot modify the adjective phrase here. |
| `The team mere agreed to revise the protocol, not to abandon it.` | `The team merely agreed to revise the protocol, not to abandon it.` | Mere is an adjective and cannot modify agreed. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- -ing adjective = cause; -ed adjective = experiencer's feeling.
- Adjective order: opinion before fact; linking verbs take adjective complements.
- Adjective describes noun/state; adverb modifies verb/adjective/adverb/clause.
- Good/well; hard/hardly; late/lately are different patterns.
- So + adjective/adverb; such + noun phrase; too = excessive; enough = sufficient.
- Quite can mean moderately or completely; pretty informal; fairly weaker.
- Comparatives: -er/more + than; as...as; much/far/a bit + comparative; the...the...; superlative + in/of.
- Verb + object stay together; place/time follow natural order.
- Mid adverb: before main verb, after be, after first auxiliary; focus adverb near target.
- Still = continuing; not any more/no longer = stopped; already = sooner than expected; yet = expected but not happened.
