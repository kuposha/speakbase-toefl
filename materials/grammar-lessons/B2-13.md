## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Наречие меняет действие, степень признака или сообщение целиком. It rose slightly описывает величину изменения; surprisingly, it rose сообщает оценку автора. Положение only определяет, какой элемент выделяется.

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

Сравни high above the roof и highly unlikely. Производное с -ly может иметь самостоятельное значение. Для сравнительной степени подойдут far или slightly; для предельного признака — completely, если смысл действительно допускает полный предел.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

После связки употребляется прилагательное: the voice sounded calm. В she answered calmly описывается действие. Enough после признака показывает достаточность для цели: wide enough to enter. Перед существительным порядок другой: enough space.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

| Contrast | Meaning |
|---|---|
| an interesting film / interested viewers | -ing causes the feeling; -ed describes the experiencer |
| a beautiful old wooden table | opinion -> size/age/colour/origin/material |
| speaks good English / speaks English well | adjective + noun vs adverb modifying verb |
| works hard / hardly works | with effort vs almost not |
| so difficult / such a difficult task | so + adjective; such + noun phrase |
| too heavy to carry / not light enough to carry | excessive degree vs insufficient degree |
| quite good / quite certain | less than very vs completely |
| much better / any better / no better | intensified comparison / question / negative comparison |
| as tall as / not as tall as | equality / inequality |
| the more you practise, the easier it becomes | linked comparative change |
| She usually works / She is usually tired | mid-position before lexical verb / after be |
| still works here / doesn't work here any more | continuing situation / changed situation |
| has already left / hasn't left yet | sooner than expected / expected but not happened by now |

### 4.5. Примеры с переводом и объяснением

| English | Перевод | Почему это подходит |
|---|---|---|
| `Only the final chapter discusses the limitations; the earlier chapters do not.` | Только в последней главе обсуждаются ограничения; в предыдущих главах они не рассматриваются. | Only directly restricts the subject/focus to the final chapter. |
| `The findings were barely statistically significant.` | Результаты едва достигли статистической значимости. | Barely is an adverb modifying the adjective phrase statistically significant. |
| `The team agreed merely to revise the protocol, not to abandon it.` | Команда лишь согласилась пересмотреть протокол, а не отказаться от него. | Merely is placed before the to-infinitive and restricts the action the team agreed to. |
| `Arguably, the evidence does not support the stronger claim.` | Вероятно, эти данные не подтверждают более сильный тезис. | Arguably is a stance adverb modifying the whole proposition. |

Примеры составлены Speakbase. Они иллюстрируют грамматическую функцию, но не подменяют индивидуальную проверку всех возможных вариантов и исключений.

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- -ing/-ed is a useful tendency, not a mechanical rule for every adjective; learn lexical patterns and complements.
- Adjective order is a strong tendency; not every combination has one rigid order. Opinion adjectives usually precede fact adjectives.
- Linking verbs take adjective complements; do not use an adverb simply because the verb is present.
- Not all adverbs end in -ly; some -ly words are adjectives. Hard/hardly and late/lately have different meanings.
- Good is an adjective; well is usually an adverb but can describe health.
- So modifies adjectives/adverbs; such introduces a noun phrase. Use such a, not a such.
- Enough follows adjectives/adverbs but precedes nouns; too/enough + to-infinitive can omit an understood object pronoun.
- Quite can mean 'moderately' or 'completely'; adjective choice and context decide. Pretty is informal; fairly is weaker.
- Some two-syllable adjectives allow both -er and more; irregular comparatives must be learned.
- Elder is mainly used before a family noun (my elder sister); older is the general comparative.
- Use in for places/groups and of for periods after superlatives; one of + superlative takes a plural noun.
- Verb and object normally stay together; do not insert an adverb between them.
- Adverb position is not free: be, one-word verbs and multi-word verb phrases have different mid-position patterns.
- Still, already and yet have different time/aspect meanings; do not substitute them mechanically.
- Focus adverb placement changes meaning (Only Maria..., Maria only..., Maria reviewed only...).

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `Even the final chapter discusses the limitations; the earlier chapters do not.` | `Only the final chapter discusses the limitations; the earlier chapters do not.` | Even adds unexpected emphasis, not the intended restriction. |
| `The findings were bare statistically significant.` | `The findings were barely statistically significant.` | Bare is an adjective and cannot modify the adjective phrase here. |
| `The team mere agreed to revise the protocol, not to abandon it.` | `The team merely agreed to revise the protocol, not to abandon it.` | Mere is an adjective and cannot modify agreed. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- -ing adjective = cause; -ed adjective = experiencer's feeling.
- Adjective order: opinion before fact; linking verbs take adjective complements.
- Adjective describes noun/state; adverb modifies verb/adjective/adverb/clause.
- Good/well; hard/hardly; late/lately are different patterns.
- So + adjective/adverb; such + noun phrase; too = excessive; enough = sufficient.
- Quite can mean moderately or completely; pretty informal; fairly weaker.
- Comparatives: -er/more + than; as...as; much/far/a bit + comparative; the...the...; superlative + in/of.
- Verb + object stay together; place/time follow natural order.
- Mid adverb: before main verb, after be, after first auxiliary; focus adverb near target.
- Still = continuing; not any more/no longer = stopped; already = sooner than expected; yet = expected but not happened.
