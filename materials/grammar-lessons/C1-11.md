## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Textual cohesion depends on clear reference chains, lexical repetition and substitution. Demonstratives, pronouns, one/ones, do so and ellipsis can connect sentences, but vague reference weakens academic argument.

Тема этого блока — **Textual reference, cohesion, substitution and ellipsis**. Измеримая цель: Поддерживать ясные связи между предложениями и абзацами без повторов и неясных местоименных ссылок

**1. Reflexive pronouns**

Myself, yourself, himself, herself, itself, ourselves, yourselves and themselves refer back to the subject when subject and object are the same: *The participants prepared themselves.* They can also add emphasis: *The director herself approved the plan.* Do not use a reflexive merely because the Russian sentence uses a reflexive verb; English verb patterns differ.

**2. One and ones**

Use **one** to replace a singular countable noun and **ones** for a plural countable noun when the noun is understood: *the first dataset was incomplete, whereas the second one was comprehensive*. They do not normally replace uncountable nouns. A determiner/adjective can precede one/ones where appropriate.

**3. So and not as clause substitutes**

Use **so** to avoid repeating an affirmative clause after verbs such as think, believe, expect, hope, suppose and imagine: *I think so; I hope so.* Use **not** after some of these verbs for a negative response: *I hope not; I don't think so.* The choice depends on the verb and the polarity of the intended meaning.

**4. Do so and such**

**Do so** replaces a repeated verb phrase, often in formal writing: *The team repeated the test, and the control group did so as well.* In informal speech, do it/do that may be more natural. **Such** can refer back to a previously mentioned kind/degree, commonly in **such + (a/an) + adjective + noun**: *It was such a difficult task that...*

**5. Ellipsis after auxiliary verbs**

When the main verb or verb phrase is already understood, it can be omitted after an auxiliary or modal: *She can analyse the data, and I can too.* Keep the auxiliary that carries tense/modality; do not repeat the whole verb phrase unnecessarily.

**6. Ellipsis of to-infinitives**

After verbs/adjectives where the infinitive is understood, **to** can remain while the rest of the infinitive is omitted: *I haven't finished, but I intend to.* This is possible only when the missing verb phrase is clear from context. Some verbs do not allow the same omission; check the pattern.

**7. Cohesion and academic reference**

Use **this/these + summary noun** (this finding, these results, this limitation) when referring to a previous proposition. Unit 103's connectors and pro-forms link ideas; Unit 104 includes reference to other work/sections. Use a precise noun head when a bare this/it/they could have more than one antecedent.

### 4.3. ??? ??????? ?????????? ?????

1. For a reflexive, check whether the object is the same person/thing as the subject or whether the pronoun is emphatic.
2. Use one/ones only when the antecedent is a countable noun and number is clear.
3. Choose so/not according to the reporting verb and whether the repeated proposition is affirmative or negative.
4. Use do so for a repeated action/verb phrase; use such when referring to a degree or kind with a noun phrase.
5. For ellipsis after an auxiliary, retain the auxiliary that carries tense/modality and omit only recoverable material.
6. For infinitive ellipsis, retain to only when the missing verb phrase is clear and the governing verb licenses the pattern.
7. In academic cohesion, add a summary noun after this/these when a bare pronoun would be vague.
8. Check every reference chain for a clear antecedent, correct number and logical link.

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- Reflexive pronouns require the correct subject/object relationship or an emphatic function; they are not a direct translation of Russian reflexive forms.
- One/ones replace countable nouns, not uncountable nouns; check singular/plural agreement.
- So/not are not interchangeable after every reporting verb; learn the patterns (think so / don't think so / hope so / hope not).
- Do so is more formal than do it/do that in many contexts and replaces a verb phrase, not a noun.
- Such normally needs a noun phrase; do not confuse it with so + adjective/adverb.
- Ellipsis is possible only when the omitted material is recoverable. Retain the auxiliary or infinitive marker required by the pattern.
- Not every verb allows to-infinitive ellipsis; check its complement pattern.
- A bare this/it/they can be ambiguous when several possible antecedents occur. Add a summary noun or repeat the precise noun.
- Connectors must express the intended relation (contrast, result, addition, concession); do not choose them only for variety.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The sample included only twelve participants. This they limits the generalisability of the results.` | `The sample included only twelve participants. This small sample size limits the generalisability of the results.` | They is a pronoun with an unclear plural antecedent in this sentence. |
| `The first dataset was incomplete, whereas the second ones was comprehensive.` | `The first dataset was incomplete, whereas the second one was comprehensive.` | Ones is plural and does not match singular dataset. |
| `The researchers repeated the procedure and the control group did one as well.` | `The researchers repeated the procedure and the control group did so as well.` | One substitutes for a noun phrase. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.
