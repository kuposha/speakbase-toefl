## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Modality expresses ability, permission, possibility, obligation, necessity, expectation and deduction. The speaker chooses a form not only for grammatical time but also for the strength of the claim and the kind of evidence available. Academic hedging then calibrates the claim so that it does not sound more certain than the evidence allows.

The source sequence in Advanced Grammar in Use Units 15?20 covers can/could/be able to, will/would/used to, may/might, must/have (got) to, needn't/don't need to/don't have to, and should/ought to/had better. Units 66?78 add adjective/adverb choices, degree, focus, comment and viewpoint expressions. Units 102 and 105 provide impersonal stance and academic discussion lead-ins.

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Ability: can, could and be able to**

- **Can** expresses present/general ability: *She can interpret the results.*
- **Could** commonly expresses general ability in the past: *He could read at four.*
- For a single successful achievement in the past, use **was/were able to** or **managed to**: *Despite the delay, the team was able to finish the analysis.*
- **Be able to** can be used in tenses and forms where can/could is unavailable.

**2. Permission and possibility**

- **Can/could/may** can ask for or give permission; could/may are often more polite.
- **Can** may describe general possibility; **may/might/could** often express a particular uncertain possibility.
- **May well** can express a fairly strong possibility; **might** can sound more tentative. Context and intonation matter.
- **May not/might not** can mean that something is possibly not true or that permission is not given; distinguish the meanings from context.

**3. Will, would and used to**

- **Will** can describe characteristic behaviour or a predictable present/future tendency.
- **Would** can describe repeated past behaviour, but not normally a past state.
- **Used to** describes past habits and past states that are no longer true: *I used to live nearby.*
- Negative/question forms normally use **didn't use to / Did ... use to?**; do not add a second past marker to used.

**4. Possibility and deduction: may/might/could; must/can't**

- **May/might/could + base verb**: possible present/future situation.
- **May/might/could have + V3**: possible past situation.
- **Must + base verb / must be**: strong positive deduction from evidence.
- **Can't be / can't have + V3**: strong negative deduction; it is not the ordinary form for a weak possibility.
- **Must have + V3** expresses a strong deduction about the past, not obligation.

**5. Obligation, necessity and absence of necessity**

- **Must** often expresses the speaker's strong requirement or an internal obligation.
- **Have to** commonly expresses an external rule or circumstance; **have got to** is frequent in informal speech.
- **Mustn't** means prohibition: do not do it.
- **Don't have to / don't need to / needn't** mean that something is not necessary. They do not mean prohibition.
- **Needn't have + V3** means the action happened but was unnecessary; **didn't need to** does not by itself tell us whether it happened.

**6. Advice, expectation and urgency**

- **Should/ought to + base verb** express advice, expectation or what is considered right.
- **Should/ought to have + V3** can express an unfulfilled expectation, regret or criticism about the past.
- **Had better + base verb** gives strong advice, often with an implied negative consequence; use **had better not** for the negative.
- Do not use *to* after should/ought to/had better in the same way as after a lexical verb: the modal pattern is followed by a base form.

**7. Hedging and stance**

- **May/might/could**, **appears/seems to**, **is likely/unlikely to**, **arguably** and **apparently** can qualify certainty.
- **Suggestive of / consistent with** express an evidential relationship, not proof of causation.
- Degree/comment adverbs such as **broadly, arguably, apparently, potentially** must be placed so their scope is clear.
- Use **it is argued/claimed/suggested that...** or **it appears that...** when the writer needs to distance the statement from an unqualified personal assertion.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Decide whether the meaning is ability, permission, possibility, obligation, absence of necessity, advice or deduction.
2. Identify the time: present/general, past habit, single past achievement, or deduction about a past event.
3. For past ability, distinguish general ability (**could**) from a specific successful achievement (**was able to/managed to**).
4. For necessity, distinguish prohibition (**mustn't**) from absence of necessity (**needn't/don't have to/don't need to**).
5. For deduction, judge the evidence strength: strong positive (**must**), strong negative (**can't**) or uncertain possibility (**may/might/could**).
6. Choose the perfect form when the inference or evaluation concerns an earlier event.
7. Add hedging only to the part of the claim that is uncertain; keep the rest of the sentence precise.
8. Check adverb position and register so the stance marker modifies the intended word or proposition.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

| Form | Core meaning | Contrast to remember |
|---|---|---|
| could swim | general past ability | was able to finish = specific successful achievement |
| mustn't leave | prohibition | don't have to leave = leaving is not necessary |
| needn't have bought it | bought it, but it was unnecessary | didn't need to buy it = necessity was absent; occurrence is not specified |
| must have left | strong deduction about the past | might have left = possible, not certain |
| can't have known | strong negative deduction about the past | may not have known = uncertain negative possibility |
| used to live here | past state/habit, no longer true | would visit often = repeated past action, not normally a past state |
| should have checked | past expectation/regret | had better check = strong advice now/for the future |
| may indicate | possibility | demonstrates = stronger claim requiring stronger evidence |
| broadly consistent | approximate degree | broadly speaking = comment on the whole proposition |

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Could normally describes general past ability; for one successful past achievement, use was/were able to or managed to.
- Would is not normally used for past states; used to can describe both past states and repeated habits.
- Mustn't is prohibition, whereas don't have to/needn't/don't need to express absence of necessity.
- Needn't have + V3 presupposes that the action happened; didn't need to does not specify this by itself.
- Must have + V3 and can't have + V3 are deductions, not past obligations.
- May/might/could have + V3 express possibility about the past; they do not automatically imply that the event occurred.
- Should have + V3 can criticise or express regret; had better is stronger and often implies a consequence.
- Hedging does not make an unsupported claim reliable. Distinguish association from causation and match the strength of the verb/adverb to the evidence.
- Adverbs can modify a word, phrase or whole clause. Check scope and position; avoid stacking several vague stance markers.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The observed association must reflect an unmeasured variable.` | `The observed association may reflect an unmeasured variable.` | Must is a strong conclusion not warranted by the cautious context. |
| `The pattern is likelihood to be associated with seasonal variation.` | `The pattern is likely to be associated with seasonal variation.` | Likelihood is a noun and does not fit be + adjective + to-infinitive. |
| `These findings are suggestive to a broader change in consumer behaviour.` | `These findings are suggestive of a broader change in consumer behaviour.` | Suggestive to is not the normal collocation in this meaning. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Ability: can (general/present), could (general past), was able to/managed to (specific past success).
- Necessity: must/have to; prohibition = mustn't; no necessity = needn't/don't have to/don't need to.
- Deduction: must (strong positive), can't (strong negative), may/might/could (possibility); perfect forms refer to the past.
- Past habit/state: used to; would mainly for repeated past actions.
- Advice: should/ought to; past criticism/regret = should have + V3; urgent advice = had better.
- Hedging: qualify the uncertain claim, distinguish possibility from proof, and place stance adverbs so their scope is clear.
