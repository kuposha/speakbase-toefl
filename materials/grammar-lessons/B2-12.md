## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced quantifiers express proportion, approximate amount, distribution and limits. Their choice depends on countability, reference set and whether the statement concerns all, most, few, neither or a proportion.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced quantifiers and determiners**. Измеримая цель: Точно использовать all/whole, few/little, either/neither, no/none, not all/not every

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Some and any**

- **Some** is common in positive statements; **any** is common in negatives and most open questions.
- Use **some** in offers/requests when expecting or inviting a positive response: *Would you like some tea?*
- Use **any** in conditional clauses and with the meaning 'it doesn't matter which': *If you need any help...; take any seat.*
- Some-/any-compounds (someone, anything, anywhere) follow similar patterns. Someone/anyone are grammatically singular, but singular **they/their** is natural when gender is unknown.

**2. No, none and no-compounds**

- **No + noun** = not a/any: *There was no bus.*
- **None** is used without a noun or in **none of + determiner/pronoun**: *None of the money; none of them.*
- **Nobody/no one/nothing/nowhere** already contain a negative meaning; do not add another negative verb: *I saw nobody*, not *I didn't see nobody*.
- With **none of + plural noun**, singular or plural verb agreement can occur; choose according to meaning/register.

**3. Much, many, a lot, plenty**

- **Much/little** with uncountable nouns; **many/few** with plural countable nouns.
- **A lot of/lots of/plenty of** with both countable plural and uncountable nouns.
- Much is less common in affirmative statements in everyday English; use a lot of more naturally.
- **Plenty of** means more than enough.

**4. Few/a few and little/a little**

- **Few/little** = not many/not much, often an insufficient amount.
- **A few/a little** = some, a small number/amount; often enough for the purpose.
- Use **fewer** with plural count nouns and **less** with uncountable nouns in standard measured comparisons: *fewer errors, less time*.
- In speech, less can occur with measures/amounts treated as a total (less than ten kilometres, less than ?20).

**5. All, most, some, any, none + noun / of**

- Use **some people, most days, all cars** without of for general reference.
- Use **some of the people, most of my time, none of them** when the set is specific or a pronoun follows.
- **All/half** can be used with or without of before a determiner: *all (of) my friends*; before a pronoun, use of: *all of us*.
- Do not say *some of people* or *most of time*.

**6. Both, either and neither**

Use these for two people/things:
- **both** = the two together; **either** = one or the other; **neither** = not one and not the other.
- **Both books**, **neither book**, **either book**; **both of the books**, **neither of them**, **either of you**.
- **Both...and**, **either...or**, **neither...nor** link parallel elements.
- After neither of, singular or plural agreement is possible; choose consistently.

**7. All, whole and every**

- **All** refers to the total quantity/group: *all the money, all my friends*.
- **Whole** means complete/entire and is mainly used with singular count nouns after a determiner: *the whole book*. Do not use whole with most uncountable nouns (*all the information*, not *the whole information*).
- **Every** means each member of a group and takes a singular noun/verb: *every student is...*; it is not normally used alone (use every one).
- **All day/week** means the complete period; **every day** means on each day.
- **Everybody/everyone/everything** are singular; use they/their naturally when referring to people.

**8. Each and every**

- **Each** focuses on individuals separately and can be used for two; it can stand alone or precede a noun.
- **Every** views members as a group and is used for three or more; it is common for frequency (*every day, every ten minutes*).
- **Each of + determiner/pronoun + plural noun** takes singular agreement in formal standard usage.
- **Every one of + determiner/pronoun + plural noun**; do not say *every of*.
- **Everyone** (one word) = everybody; **every one** (two words) refers to each individual item/person.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Identify whether the noun is countable singular, countable plural or uncountable.
2. Decide whether the statement is positive, negative, an open question, an offer/request or a conditional; choose some/any accordingly.
3. Use no + noun, none (of) or a no-compound without adding a second negative.
4. Choose much/many and little/few by countability; use a lot of/plenty of when a neutral affirmative expression is needed.
5. Decide whether few/little means 'not enough' or a few/a little means 'some'.
6. Use less with uncountable quantities and fewer with plural count nouns; treat measures as units where appropriate.
7. Decide whether the set is general or specific before choosing noun alone vs of + determiner/pronoun.
8. Use both/either/neither only for two; use all/any/none for larger groups.
9. Distinguish whole (complete singular count noun) from all (total quantity) and every (each member/frequency).
10. Distinguish each (individual focus) from every (group/frequency); check singular agreement and every one/everyone spelling.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

| Form | Meaning | Example |
|---|---|---|
| some | an amount/person expected or known to exist | Would you like some coffee? |
| any | open possibility / no matter which | Do you have any questions? Take any seat. |
| no + noun | not a/any | There was no bus. |
| none (of) | not one/any from a set | None of the tickets was/were valid. |
| few / little | not many/not much; often insufficient | Few people agreed; little time remained. |
| a few / a little | some; a small amount/number | A few people agreed; a little time remained. |
| less / fewer | uncountable amount / countable number | less time; fewer mistakes |
| all / whole | total quantity / complete single item | all the information; the whole book |
| every / each | group/frequency / individual focus | every student; each student |
| everyone / every one | everybody / each item/person individually | Everyone arrived; every one of the files was checked |

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Some is possible in questions when offering/requesting or when the speaker expects the item exists; any is common in open questions and negatives.
- Any can mean 'no matter which' and is not always negative.
- Someone/anyone/everyone are grammatically singular but can take singular they/their.
- No + noun and no-compounds already express negation; avoid double negatives in standard English.
- None of + plural noun can take singular or plural agreement; context and register matter.
- Much is less natural in many affirmative everyday statements; a lot of is usually more idiomatic.
- Few/little and a few/a little differ in whether the amount is insufficient or simply small.
- Fewer is standard with plural count nouns; less is standard with uncountable nouns, though less is also used with measures/amounts viewed as a unit.
- Use of before pronouns and specific determiners in most of/some of/none of; omit of for general plural nouns.
- Both/either/neither refer to two; all/any/none usually refer to more than two.
- Whole normally modifies a singular count noun with a determiner; all is used with uncountable nouns.
- Every takes a singular noun and verb; each can be used for two and can stand alone.
- Everyone (one word) means everybody; every one (two words) refers to each member of a set.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `There is a great number of evidence to support the claim, so no conclusion can be drawn.` | `There is hardly any evidence to support the claim, so no conclusion can be drawn.` | A great number of requires a plural countable noun. |
| `A large number of applications was received after the deadline yesterday.` | `A large number of applications were received after the deadline yesterday.` | Was incorrectly treats number as the singular head in this expression. |
| `A great deal of research have been devoted to this question.` | `A great deal of research has been devoted to this question.` | Have is plural and does not agree with the uncountable noun phrase. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Some: positive/offer/request; any: negative/open question/conditional/'no matter which'.
- No + noun; none (of) without a noun; nobody/nothing/nowhere already negative.
- Much/little = uncountable; many/few = plural countable; a lot/plenty = both.
- Few/little = not enough; a few/a little = some.
- Less + uncountable; fewer + plural count noun (measures may use less).
- General set: most people; specific set/pronoun: most of the people/most of them.
- Both/either/neither = two; all/any/none = larger groups.
- All = total; whole = complete singular count noun; every = each member/frequency.
- Each focuses on individuals (can be two); every views a group; each of/every one of + plural set.
- Everyone = everybody; every one = each item/person.
