## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced condition markers express restrictions, sufficient conditions, unexpected conditions and hypothetical framing. They differ in meaning and in the clause or phrase they introduce.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced condition markers**. Измеримая цель: Использовать unless/provided/as long as/in case/even if и выбирать подходящий смысловой оттенок

### 4.2. ???????????? ? ?????????????? ??????

**1. In case + clause: precaution**

- *Take a map in case you get lost.*
- *I'll write down the password in case I forget it.*
- *I gave her my number in case she needed to contact me.*

In case explains why someone takes action now: something may happen later, so preparation is made in advance. It can refer to a past precaution too. Do not normally use **will** after in case for a future possibility: *in case I forget*, not *in case I will forget*.

**2. In case of + noun**

- *In case of fire, leave the building immediately.*
- *In case of emergency, call this number.*

This fixed prepositional phrase means 'if there is...' and is common in notices/instructions. It is followed by a noun phrase, not a finite clause.

**3. In case vs if**

- *I'll take an umbrella in case it rains.* (I take it now as a precaution.)
- *I'll take an umbrella if it rains.* (I will take it only if rain occurs.)

The difference is the timing and reason for the action, not simply the probability.

**4. Unless = except if / if not**

- *You can't enter unless you are a member.*
- *Unless we leave now, we'll be late.*
- *Unless* already contains a negative meaning; avoid adding another negative that reverses or obscures the condition.

You can often replace unless with **if ... not**, but check that the sentence meaning remains the same.

**5. As long as / so long as / provided (that) / providing (that)**

- *You can borrow my car as long as you promise to drive carefully.*
- *Travelling by car is convenient provided that you have somewhere to park.*
- *Providing the weather is good, we'll have a picnic.*

These forms introduce a condition or requirement. They are not precaution phrases: the result depends on the condition being satisfied.

**6. Future reference after condition markers**

After unless, as long as, so long as, provided and providing, use a present tense for a future condition:
- *I'll go unless it rains.*
- *We'll have a picnic provided the weather is good.*

Do not use will in the conditional clause in this ordinary future pattern.

**7. Related conditional patterns**

Advanced conditional forms may use **should + subject + base verb**, **had + subject + V3**, or **were + subject + to-infinitive** in formal inversion. These are covered in B2-01/C1-02; this lesson focuses on the meaning of condition markers and precaution.

### 4.3. ??? ??????? ?????????? ?????

1. Ask whether the action is taken now as preparation for a possible event: use in case.
2. If the action will happen only if the condition is met, use if.
3. For a negative condition meaning 'except if', use unless or if ... not; avoid double negatives.
4. For a requirement that must be satisfied, use as long as/so long as/provided/providing.
5. Use in case of + noun phrase in notices and instructions; do not follow it with a finite clause.
6. For future meaning after unless/as long as/provided/providing, use a present tense, not will.
7. Check whether the sentence describes precaution, permission/requirement or a result dependent on a condition.

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- In case is a precaution marker, not a simple synonym of if.
- Do not normally use will after in case, unless, as long as, so long as, provided or providing in an ordinary future conditional clause.
- In case of is followed by a noun phrase: *in case of emergency*, not *in case of something happens*.
- Unless already means except if/if not; avoid an additional negative unless the intended meaning is carefully controlled.
- Provided/providing and as long as make the result dependent on a condition; they do not express precaution.
- Even if is concessive/hypothetical: the result is unchanged even if the condition occurs.
- Do not confuse in case + clause with in case of + noun phrase.
- Use a full clause after unless/provided/as long as; use a noun phrase after in case of.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `You can borrow the equipment even if you return it by Friday.` | `You can borrow the equipment provided that you return it by Friday.` | Even if means the result remains true regardless of the condition. |
| `Take a printed map unless your phone battery dies.` | `Take a printed map in case your phone battery dies.` | Unless means if not and changes the logic. |
| `Provided that the cost increases, the project will continue.` | `Even if the cost increases, the project will continue.` | Provided that makes the condition a requirement for the result. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.
