## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Mixed Conditional связывает условие и результат, относящиеся к разным временным точкам: прошлое условие может объяснять настоящее состояние, а гипотетическое настоящее — прошлый результат. Wish/if only выражают сожаление или желание изменить факт.

Тема этого блока — **Mixed Conditionals, wish and if only**. Измеримая цель: Связывать прошлое условие с настоящим результатом; выражать сожаление и нереальность

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Unreal present/future: if + Past Simple, would + base verb**

- If I had more time, I would learn another language.
- If she were here, she would know what to do.
- If I were you, I wouldn't buy that coat.

The past form signals distance from reality, not past time. **Were** is common for all persons in formal hypothetical clauses; **was** is also used informally with I/he/she/it in many varieties. The result clause normally uses would/could/might + base verb.

**2. Unreal past: if + Past Perfect, would/could/might have + V3**

- If I had known about the meeting, I would have attended.
- If they hadn't been so tired, they might have gone out.

This describes a past condition that did not happen and its imagined past result. The short form 'd can mean had in the if-clause or would in the result clause; identify it by the verb form that follows.

**3. Mixed conditional: past condition, present result**

- If she had taken the earlier train, she would be here now.
- If they had booked a table, we wouldn't be waiting in the queue now.

The condition is unreal in the past (Past Perfect), but the result is a present state/situation (would + base verb).

**4. Mixed conditional: present condition, past result**

- If I were more organised, I wouldn't have missed the deadline yesterday.
- If he knew how to drive, he would have driven us to the station.

The condition describes an unreal present characteristic/state; the result refers to a past event (would/could/might have + V3).

**5. Wish/if only for present unreality**

- I wish I knew the answer. (I do not know it.)
- If only I had more time!
- I wish I didn't have to work tomorrow.

Use **wish/if only + Past Simple** for a present situation that is different from what the speaker wants. If only is more emphatic.

**6. Wish/if only for past regret**

- I wish I had checked the date.
- If only we hadn't missed the train!

Use **wish/if only + Past Perfect** to express regret about something that happened or did not happen in the past.

**7. Wish + could / could have**

- I wish I could help you. (I cannot help.)
- I wish I could have gone to the party. (I was unable to go.)

Could expresses ability/possibility that is absent; could have refers to an unrealised past opportunity.

**8. Wish + would for a desired change or complaint**

- I wish it would stop raining.
- I wish you wouldn't keep interrupting me.

Use **wish + would + base verb** when the speaker wants a situation or another person's behaviour to change, often with irritation. Do not use it simply to describe a desired state: *I wish I had a car* (not *I wish I would have a car*). Do not use *wish + would have* for past regret.

**9. Wish vs hope**

Use **wish somebody luck/all the best** for a conventional good wish; use **hope + present clause** for a desired future outcome: *I hope you feel better soon*, not *I wish you feel better soon*.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Mark the time of the condition and the time of the result separately.
2. Unreal present/future condition ? if + Past Simple; result ? would/could/might + base verb.
3. Unreal past condition ? if + Past Perfect; past result ? would/could/might have + V3.
4. For a mixed conditional, choose the result form by its actual time: present result uses would + base verb; past result uses would have + V3.
5. For present regret use wish/if only + Past Simple; for past regret use wish/if only + Past Perfect.
6. Use wish + could for inability now and wish + could have for a missed past possibility.
7. Use wish + would for a desired change/complaint, not for a static state or past regret.
8. Do not put would in an ordinary if-clause; keep would/could/might in the result clause.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

If I had studied medicine, I would be a doctor now (past→present). If I were more careful, I wouldn't have made that mistake (present→past). I wish I had checked the date (past regret).

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Past Simple after if/wish can refer to the present/future; do not interpret it automatically as past time.
- In hypothetical if/wish clauses, were is formal and widely used; was is also possible in informal usage with some subjects.
- Do not normally use would in the if-clause: *If I knew*, not *If I would know*. The lesson does not cover rare polite-willingness uses as the core pattern.
- In mixed conditionals, do not force the same tense into both clauses; select each form by the time of the condition/result.
- Distinguish had from would in 'd forms: had is followed by V3; would is followed by a base verb or have + V3.
- Wish + Past Simple = present unreality; wish + Past Perfect = past regret.
- Wish + would expresses a desired change/complaint; it is not a substitute for wish + Past Simple or wish + Past Perfect.
- Wish + could/could have expresses inability or unrealised possibility, not the same meaning as wish + would.
- Use hope, not wish, for a desired outcome in a finite present/future clause (I hope you feel better).

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `If she had taken the earlier train, she would have been yesterday here now.` | `If she had taken the earlier train, she would be here now.` | Would have been places the result in the past, not now. |
| `If I were more organised, I wouldn't miss now the deadline yesterday.` | `If I were more organised, I wouldn't have missed the deadline yesterday.` | Wouldn't miss describes a present/future result, not yesterday. |
| `I wish I read tomorrow the instructions before starting the task.` | `I wish I had read the instructions before starting the task.` | Read tomorrow does not express past regret. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

- Mixed: past condition + present result; or present condition + past result.
- Wish/if only + Past Simple = present regret/unreality; + Past Perfect = past regret.
- Wish + could/could have = inability or missed possibility.
