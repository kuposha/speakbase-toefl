## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Ellipsis omits words that are recoverable from context, while substitution replaces a repeated phrase with one, do, so, not or another pro-form. These devices reduce repetition without making reference unclear.

Тема этого блока — **Ellipsis, substitution and pronoun reference**. Измеримая цель: Избегать ненужных повторов, использовать one/ones/so/neither/do и ясные ссылки

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Reflexive pronouns**

Use myself/yourself/himself/herself/itself/ourselves/yourselves/themselves when the object is the same person/thing as the subject: She introduced herself. They can also add emphasis: The director herself approved it. English reflexive patterns are lexical; do not translate Russian reflexive verbs mechanically.

**2. One and ones**

Use **one** for a singular countable noun and **ones** for plural countable nouns when the noun is understood: I prefer the blue one; the smaller ones are cheaper. They do not normally replace uncountable nouns.

**3. So and not as clause substitutes**

After selected verbs (think, believe, hope, expect, suppose, imagine), **so** can replace an affirmative clause and **not** can replace a negative clause: I think so; I hope not; I don't think so. The choice depends on the governing verb and intended polarity.

**4. Do so and such**

**Do so** replaces a repeated verb phrase, especially in formal writing: The team repeated the test, and the control group did so too. **Such** can refer back to a type/degree, often in **such + (a/an) + adjective + noun**: It was such a difficult task that... Distinguish **such + noun phrase** from **so + adjective/adverb**.

**5. Ellipsis after auxiliaries**

When the main verb is recoverable, omit it after an auxiliary/modal: She can analyse the data, and I can too. Retain the auxiliary that carries tense/modality; do not repeat the whole verb phrase.

**6. Ellipsis of to-infinitives**

After verbs/adjectives where the infinitive is understood, **to** can remain while the rest is omitted: I haven't finished, but I intend to. This is possible only when the missing action is clear and the governing verb licenses the pattern.

**7. Cohesion and reference**

Use this/these + summary noun to refer clearly to a previous idea: this finding, these results, this limitation. Pronouns and substitutes need a clear antecedent, matching number and a recoverable meaning.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. For a reflexive, check whether subject and object are the same or whether the pronoun is emphatic.
2. Use one/ones only when the antecedent is a countable noun and number is clear.
3. Choose so/not according to the governing verb and polarity.
4. Use do so to replace a verb phrase; use such + noun phrase to refer to a kind/degree.
5. For auxiliary ellipsis, retain the auxiliary that carries tense/modality and omit only recoverable material.
6. For infinitive ellipsis, retain to only when the omitted verb phrase is clear and the governing verb permits it.
7. Add a summary noun after this/these when a bare pronoun would be ambiguous.
8. Check antecedent, number, register and logical connection.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

I prefer the blue one (noun substitution). She promised to revise it and did so (verb phrase). I think so / I hope not (clause substitution).

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Reflexives require subject/object identity or emphasis; they are not a direct translation of Russian reflexive forms.
- One/ones replace countable nouns, not uncountable nouns.
- So/not are not interchangeable after every verb; learn the governing patterns.
- Do so is more formal than do it/do that in many contexts and replaces a verb phrase, not a noun.
- Such normally introduces a noun phrase; so modifies an adjective/adverb.
- Ellipsis is possible only when the omitted material is recoverable; retain the auxiliary or to marker required by the pattern.
- Not every verb allows infinitive ellipsis; check its complement pattern.
- Bare this/it/they can be ambiguous when several antecedents are possible; add a summary noun or repeat the noun.
- Avoid ellipsis when it makes the sentence difficult to understand.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `I don't like the red folder. I'll take the blue ones.` | `I don't like the red folder. I'll take the blue one.` | Ones is plural and would refer to multiple folders. |
| `The first proposal was expensive, and the second ones was impractical.` | `The first proposal was expensive, and the second one was impractical.` | Ones is plural, but the antecedent proposal is singular. |
| `She promised to check the figures and did one before the meeting.` | `She promised to check the figures and did so before the meeting.` | Did one is not a standard substitute for a verb phrase. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Reflexive = subject/object identity or emphasis.
- One/ones replace singular/plural countable nouns.
- So/not substitute for a proposition after selected verbs.
- Do so replaces a verb phrase; such + noun phrase refers to a type/degree.
- Ellipsis after auxiliaries keeps the auxiliary; infinitive ellipsis can retain to.
- Use this/these + summary noun when a bare reference is unclear.
- Check antecedent, number, governing verb and recoverability.
