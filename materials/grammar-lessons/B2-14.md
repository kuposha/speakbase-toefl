## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced concession and contrast structures express an unexpected relationship between propositions. The connector determines clause structure, word order and emphasis.

Тема этого блока — **Concession, contrast and complex linking**. Цель: точно выбирать уступительные и контрастные связки, различать finite clauses и noun phrases, отделять факт от гипотезы, контролировать позицию клаузы и регистр.

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Factual concession: although, though, even though**

- **Although/though/even though + finite clause**: Although the traffic was bad, we arrived on time.
- **Though** can appear at the end: The house isn't very nice. I like the garden, though.
- **Even though** gives stronger emphasis to a factual contrast.

**2. Sentence-level contrast: however, nevertheless and even so**

- **However/nevertheless/even so** are conjunctive adverbs: they connect ideas across clauses/sentences but do not grammatically introduce a subordinate clause.
- Use a full stop or semicolon before them and normally a comma after: *The method was expensive; nevertheless, it was adopted widely.*
- **However** can also mean "no matter how" before an adjective/adverb: *However carefully we checked, some uncertainty remained.* This is a different construction.
- Do not use *however + subject + verb* as a direct substitute for *although*: use *Although the method was expensive, it was adopted widely*.

**3. Despite and in spite of**

- **Despite/in spite of + noun/pronoun/-ing**: Despite the rain, we went out; in spite of feeling tired, she continued.
- Use **despite**, not *despite of*.
- **In spite of the fact (that) / despite the fact (that) + clause** is possible.
- Do not put a finite clause directly after despite/in spite of.

**4. Reason: because, because of, as, since, seeing that, for**

- **Because/as/since/seeing that + clause** gives a reason. Because often introduces the most direct reason; as/since can present a reason already known or less central.
- **Because of + noun/-ing**: The match was cancelled because of the rain.
- **For + clause** is a coordinating conjunction in formal/literary style and usually adds an explanation after the main statement (*The road was closed, for repairs were under way*). It is not interchangeable with because in every position.
- **With + noun phrase** can introduce a circumstance that explains a result: *With so many people absent, the meeting was postponed*. This is a prepositional phrase, not a subordinating conjunction.

**5. Condition and precaution**

- **In case + clause** = take a precaution because something may happen: Take an umbrella in case it rains.
- **If + clause** = action/result depends on the condition: I'll take an umbrella if it rains.
- **In case of + noun**: In case of fire, leave the building.
- **Unless** = except if/if not; **as long as/so long as/provided/providing (that)** introduce a required condition.
- Use present tense for future conditions after these markers, not will.

**6. As, like and as if/as though**

- **As + clause** can mean 'in the way that' or introduce a reason/time clause.
- **Like + noun/pronoun** is common in comparisons: He looks like his father. In informal speech, like can introduce a clause; as is more formal/standard in careful writing.
- **As if/as though + clause** describes appearance or an unreal/imagined comparison: He talks as if he knows everything; she looked as though she had seen a ghost.
- Choose tense according to whether the comparison is real, unreal present or unreal past.

**7. Time: during, for, while, as, when, until and by**

- **During + noun** refers to a period/event; **for + duration** tells how long.
- **While/as + clause** describes simultaneous actions; **when** identifies the time an event happens.
- **Until** marks continuation up to a point; **by** marks a deadline/no later than a point.
- After ordinary time conjunctions with future meaning, use a present tense rather than will: *I'll call you when I arrive*. Will can occur in a time clause when it expresses willingness or insistence, not a simple future prediction.

**8. Sequence and rapid succession**

- **Before/after/once/as soon as** establish event sequence.
- **Hardly/scarcely ... when** and **no sooner ... than** express rapid succession; when the negative adverbial is fronted, the first clause requires subject-auxiliary inversion.
- **Not until** can be fronted for emphasis and then triggers inversion in the main clause.

**9. Purpose and result**

- **To/in order to/so as to + infinitive** express purpose; so as to is more formal.
- **So that/in order that + clause** express purpose and can include can/could/will/would according to time.
- **So + adjective/adverb ... that** and **such + noun phrase ... that** express result.
- **For + noun** can express purpose/function (a tool for cutting); for + -ing can describe function, not necessarily intention.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Identify the relation: concession, reason, condition/precaution, comparison, time/sequence, purpose or result.
2. For concession, use although/even though + clause or despite/in spite of + noun/-ing.
3. For reason, choose because/as/since + clause or because of + noun/-ing; for is formal and explanatory.
4. For precaution, use in case; for a condition controlling the result, use if/unless/provided/as long as.
5. Choose as/like/as if by checking whether the following element is a clause, noun phrase or unreal comparison.
6. Use during + noun, for + duration, while/as + clause, by + deadline and until + continuation point.
7. For future time clauses, use present tense after when/as soon as/until/before/after.
8. For purpose, choose to/in order to/so as to or so that + clause; for result, use so...that or such...that.
9. If hardly/no sooner/not until is fronted, check the required inversion.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

**Concession with an accepted fact:** Although / Even though Matt does not speak Spanish, I think he should still visit Madrid.
**Hypothetical condition:** Even if Matt does not speak Spanish, I think he should still visit Madrid.
**Contrast between two situations:** Juan gets lots of homework from school, whereas Mia gets very little.
**Concessive while:** While the sample was small, the findings were consistent.
**Concessive adjective fronting:** Strange though it may seem, the result is consistent.

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Although/though/even though take a clause; despite/in spite of take a noun phrase or -ing, not a finite clause directly.
- Despite has no following of; use in spite of.
- Because/as/since/seeing that differ in information focus/register; do not replace them mechanically.
- In case is precaution, not a synonym of if; in case of takes a noun phrase.
- Unless already means except if/if not; avoid unintended double negatives.
- As and like differ by construction and register; like + clause is common informally, while as + clause is standard in formal writing.
- As if/as though can describe a real appearance or an unreal comparison; tense signals the intended distance.
- During takes a noun phrase; while/as take a clause. For expresses duration; by marks a deadline; until marks continuation. Concessive while expresses an unexpected contrast; contrastive while compares two situations.
- Do not use will in ordinary future time/condition clauses after when/until/as soon as/unless/provided.
- Hardly/scarcely pair with when; no sooner pairs with than.
- Purpose infinitives and result clauses are not interchangeable; check whether the clause states intention or consequence.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `Despite the high cost, the method was adopted widely.` | Несмотря на высокую стоимость, метод получил широкое распространение. | Despite is followed by a noun phrase, not a finite clause. |
| `Despite the method was expensive, it was adopted widely.` | `Although the method was expensive, it was adopted widely.` | Despite cannot introduce a finite clause directly. |
| `However the method was expensive, it was adopted widely.` | `Although the method was expensive, it was adopted widely.` | However is a conjunctive adverb, not a subordinating conjunction; use although to introduce the finite concessive clause. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.9. Академический английский и TOEFL-перенос

In academic reading, identify whether the linker introduces a clause or noun phrase and track contrast. In writing, use concession to qualify a claim without weakening its logic.

- **Reading:** синтаксические связи, reference, modifiers и логические отношения.
- **Listening:** tense/aspect, weak forms, modality, speaker stance и связи между идеями.
- **Writing — Build a Sentence:** точность структуры, согласование, порядок слов и verb patterns.
- **Writing — Email / Academic Discussion:** ясность, appropriate register, аргументация и cohesion.
- **Speaking — Listen and Repeat / Take an Interview:** точность воспроизведения формы и самостоятельное применение.

### 4.10. Примеры для управляемого разбора

- We know that it rained a lot. ___ it rained a lot, they had a good time. (Although / Even if) Answer: Although.
- ___ the rain, they had a good time. (Despite / In spite of) Answer: In spite of.
- We know Matt does not speak Spanish. ___ Matt does not speak Spanish, he should still visit Madrid. (Even though / Even if) Answer: Even though.
- We do not know whether Matt speaks Spanish. ___ he does not speak Spanish, he should still visit Madrid. (Even though / Even if) Answer: Even if.
- The diesel model of the car is more expensive, but it is better value for money. Rewrite with while/whereas. Suggested answer: The diesel model of the car, while more expensive, is better value for money.
- ___ the diesel model is more expensive, it is better value for money. (Although / While) Answer: While.

Для каждого примера назови целевой элемент, объясни форму, укажи, почему отвлекающий вариант неверен, и придумай собственное предложение с тем же правилом.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Although/even though + clause; despite/in spite of + noun/-ing.
- Because/as/since + clause; because of + noun/-ing.
- In case = precaution; if = condition; unless = except if; provided/as long as = required condition.
- As + clause; like + noun (or informal clause); as if/as though = appearance/unreal comparison.
- During + noun; for + duration; while/as + clause; by = deadline; until = continuation.
- Future time/condition clauses take present tense, not will.
- Purpose: to/in order to/so as to; so that + clause.
- Result: so + adjective/adverb + that; such + noun phrase + that.
- Hardly/scarcely...when; no sooner...than; fronted negative/time expressions may trigger inversion. However/nevertheless/even so connect independent clauses and take sentence-level punctuation.
