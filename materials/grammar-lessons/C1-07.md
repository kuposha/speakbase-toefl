### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced passive structures include impersonal reporting, have/get causatives and complex tense/modal combinations. The choice determines whether the agent, affected object or reported proposition is foregrounded.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced passive, impersonal and causative structures**. Измеримая цель: Выбирать пассивные и безличные модели для нейтрального академического регистра

**1. Choosing a passive**

Use the passive when the affected person/thing or the process is more important than the agent, or when the agent is unknown, obvious or irrelevant. Keep the correct tense and aspect in the auxiliary; the main verb is V3.

**2. Passive with two objects**

Some verbs allow two passive forms:
- Active: *They gave the researcher an award.*
- Passive: *The researcher was given an award.*
- Passive: *An award was given to the researcher.*

The choice of passive subject changes the focus. With some verbs, only one passive pattern is natural; check the verb's object/complement frame.

**3. Passive with object + complement**

- Active: *They elected her chairperson.*
- Passive: *She was elected chairperson.*

The complement remains after the passive verb; do not insert an unnecessary article or preposition.

**4. Passive with verb + -ing / to-infinitive**

- Active: *They saw the money climbing over the fence.*
- Passive: *The money was seen climbing over the fence.*
- Active: *They expect the plan to succeed.*
- Passive: *The plan is expected to succeed.*

Some verbs take an object + -ing or object + to-infinitive in the active; in the passive, the object becomes the subject and the complement pattern changes accordingly. Passive perception verbs commonly use a to-infinitive where the active uses a bare infinitive.

**5. Transitive multi-word verbs**

A transitive multi-word verb can form a passive when it takes an object: *The problem was dealt with; the patient was looked after.* Keep the particle/preposition with the verb. Do not separate a preposition from its object.

**6. Impersonal reporting passive**

- *It is said that the building is unsafe.*
- *The building is said to be unsafe.*
- *The manuscript is thought to have been written in the fifteenth century.*

Use **It + passive reporting verb + that-clause** or **subject + passive reporting verb + to-infinitive**. Use **to have + V3** when the reported event is earlier than the reporting point.

**7. Causative have/get**

- *She had the device repaired.*
- *They got the technician to inspect the system.*

**Have/get + object + V3** means arranging for something to be done to the object. **Get + person + to-infinitive** means persuading/arranging for a person to act. Distinguish both from *have someone do something*.

**8. Modal perfect passive**

Use **modal + have been + V3** for a modal meaning about an earlier passive event: *The results may have been corrupted; the data should have been checked.*

### 4.3. ??? ??????? ?????????? ?????

1. Identify the active verb frame: one object, two objects, object + complement, object + -ing or object + infinitive.
2. Decide what should become the passive subject and whether the verb allows that passive.
3. Keep the original tense/aspect in the auxiliary and use V3 for the lexical verb.
4. Preserve the complement or particle required by the verb.
5. For reporting, choose it + passive + that-clause or subject + passive reporting verb + infinitive.
6. Use to have + V3 when the reported event precedes the reporting point.
7. For causatives, decide whether the object receives an action (have/get + object + V3) or a person is arranged/persuaded to act (get + person + to-infinitive).
8. Check that the passive does not accidentally change the agent, affected participant or time relationship.

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- Not every verb or multi-word verb can be passivised; the verb must have a suitable object/complement frame.
- Some verbs allow two passive forms, but not all do so equally naturally. Do not force a second passive when the source pattern does not license it.
- Keep object complements after the passive verb where required (elected chairperson, considered reliable).
- With passive perception patterns, the infinitive marker to may appear where the active pattern uses a bare infinitive.
- In a passive multi-word verb, keep the particle/preposition: looked after, dealt with. Do not strand or omit it.
- Impersonal reporting is a reporting structure, not proof that the reported statement is true.
- To have + V3 marks an earlier event; to be + V3 describes a simultaneous/current passive state or event.
- Have/get something done differs from have someone do something and get someone to do something.
- Modal perfect passive requires modal + have been + V3; do not omit been.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The new treatment is believed to have be effective in early trials.` | `The new treatment is believed to be effective in early trials.` | To have be is not a grammatical perfect infinitive; it needs been. |
| `The ancient manuscript is thought to be wrote in the fifteenth century.` | `The ancient manuscript is thought to have been written in the fifteenth century.` | To be wrote has an incorrect participle form and does not show the earlier action. |
| `It expects that the building will reopen next spring.` | `It is expected that the building will reopen next spring.` | Expects is active and would require an agent subject. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.
