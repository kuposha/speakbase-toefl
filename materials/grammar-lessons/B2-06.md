## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced causative patterns distinguish arranging a service, persuading or requiring another person to act, and causing a state/change. The complement after have/get/make/let differs by construction.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced causative and linking verbs**. Измеримая цель: Различать have/get causative, linking verbs и complex complements

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Have/get something done: arranging a service**

- *Lisa had the roof repaired.*
- *When are you going to get your hair cut?*

**Have/get + object + V3** means the subject arranges for someone else to do the work. Keep the order: have/get + object + past participle.

**2. Have/get something done: an unwanted experience**

- *They had their bags stolen while travelling.*
- *Gary had his nose broken in a fight.*

The same form can describe something that happens to someone or their belongings; it does not mean they arranged it. Context distinguishes the two meanings.

**3. Have someone do / get someone to do / make someone do**

- **Have + person + bare infinitive**: *I had the assistant print the report.*
- **Get + person + to-infinitive**: *We got the supplier to replace the equipment.*
- **Make + person + bare infinitive**: *The delay made passengers wait.*
- Passive **be made + to-infinitive**: *They were made to wait.*

Do not mix the bare infinitive and to-infinitive patterns.

**4. Linking verbs and subject complements**

Linking verbs connect the subject to a description/identity rather than taking an ordinary object:
- **be, seem, appear, become, remain, stay** + adjective/noun phrase;
- **get, go, turn, grow** can describe a change of state (*go blind, turn red, grow tired*).
- **look, sound, smell, taste, feel** can be linking verbs + adjective (*The soup tastes salty*) or action verbs + adverb/object in another meaning: *She looked carefully at the label* (action + adverb), but *She looked tired* (linking verb + adjective).
- **Get** can be a linking verb meaning become (*get tired*), while **get + person + to-infinitive** is causative. **Go/turn** often occur in conventional change-of-state combinations (*go wrong, turn pale*); these combinations are partly lexical and should be learned as collocations.

Use an adjective or noun phrase as a subject complement: *The proposal seems reasonable*, not *seems reasonably*.

**5. Verb + object/complement patterns**

Some verbs take:
- object + adjective/noun complement: *They considered the plan impractical; they elected her chairperson*;
- object + preposition + -ing: *prevent someone from leaving; accuse someone of cheating*;
- object + to-infinitive: *persuade someone to apply*;
- object + bare infinitive after selected verbs (make, let, and some perception verbs).

Learn the verb with its complete complement frame.

**6. Verbs with two objects**

- *She gave me the report.*
- *She gave the report to me.*
- *He bought her a present.*
- *He bought a present for her.*

Some verbs allow both patterns; some prefer or require **to** or **for**; some have a meaning difference. Compare *give someone a book / give a book to someone* and *buy someone a gift / buy a gift for someone*. Other verbs require a preposition: *explain the rule to someone*, not normally *explain someone the rule*. Do not assume every verb permits either order.

**7. Perception and -ing/to-infinitive patterns**

After see/hear/watch/notice/feel:
- object + bare infinitive often presents the whole event: *I saw the cyclist fall* (the fall as a complete event);
- object + -ing presents an action in progress or a partial view: *I saw the cyclist falling* (I witnessed the action in progress; completion is not asserted).
The -ing form does not necessarily mean the speaker saw the entire action, and the bare infinitive does not always imply that every stage was observed; context determines the intended viewpoint.

Other verbs take an -ing or to-infinitive complement according to their lexical pattern. A change can affect meaning/aspect; consult the verb's pattern rather than translating directly.

**8. Linking verbs with get/go/turn/become**

Some linking verbs describe gradual or sudden change: *become aware, get tired, go wrong, turn pale*. Certain combinations are fixed collocations; not every adjective can follow every linking verb.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. If the subject arranges a service, use have/get + object + V3; if the subject experiences an unwanted event, the same form may describe what happened to them.
2. If a person is made/asked/persuaded to act, choose have + person + bare infinitive, get + person + to-infinitive or make + person + bare infinitive.
3. For a linking verb, identify whether the complement describes the subject; use an adjective/noun phrase, not an adverb.
4. Check whether get/go/turn/become expresses a change of state and whether the combination is a fixed collocation.
5. For two-object verbs, decide whether the person or thing should be the first object and whether to/for is required.
6. With perception verbs, choose bare infinitive for the whole event or -ing for an action in progress.
7. For other complex complements, identify the governing verb and select its licensed object + preposition/-ing/to-infinitive pattern.
8. Check the passive form separately: make + object + bare infinitive becomes be made + to-infinitive.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

I had the assistant print it. I got the assistant to print it. I had the document printed. The assistant was made to print it.

| Что проверяем | Вопрос для выбора |
|---|---|
| Форма | Какие элементы обязательны и в каком порядке они стоят? |
| Значение | Что именно меняется: время, длительность, реальность, степень уверенности или фокус? |
| Контекст | Что уже известно собеседнику и каков регистр высказывания? |
| Проверка | Если заменить форму близкой конструкцией, изменится ли смысл? Объясни, почему. |

### 4.5. Примеры с переводом и объяснением

| English | Перевод | Почему это подходит |
|---|---|---|---|
| `The director had the assistant prepare the minutes.` | Руководитель поручил ассистенту подготовить протокол. | Have + object + bare infinitive: had the assistant prepare. |
| `We got the supplier to replace the damaged equipment.` | Нам удалось убедить поставщика заменить повреждённое оборудование. | Get + object + to-infinitive expresses persuasion/arrangement. |
| `The delay made the passengers wait for another hour.` | Задержка заставила пассажиров ждать ещё час. | Make + object + bare infinitive in active voice. |
| `The employees were made to read the safety procedure again.` | Сотрудников заставили ещё раз прочитать правила безопасности. | Passive make requires to-infinitive: were made to read. |

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Have/get + object + V3 has both an arranged-service meaning and an experience meaning; the subject does not always arrange the event.
- Get + person + to-infinitive differs from have/make + person + bare infinitive.
- In the passive, make requires to-infinitive: *They were made to wait*.
- Linking verbs take subject complements; use adjectives/nouns rather than adverbs in that role.
- Some verbs can be both linking and action verbs (look, feel, smell, taste, sound); the complement type and meaning determine the analysis.
- Two-object alternation is lexical: not every verb allows both to/for patterns.
- Verb + object + preposition patterns must be learned as units; do not omit the preposition or replace it mechanically.
- With perception verbs, bare infinitive and -ing can differ in aspect; neither form is a universal substitute for the other.
- Some adjective/noun complements after linking verbs are fixed collocations; do not generalise a pattern to every adjective.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The director had the assistant to prepare the minutes.` | `The director had the assistant prepare the minutes.` | To prepare is used after get + object, not have + object in this causative pattern. |
| `We got the supplier replace the damaged equipment.` | `We got the supplier to replace the damaged equipment.` | Bare replace is not the complement after got + object. |
| `The delay made the passengers to wait for another hour.` | `The delay made the passengers wait for another hour.` | To wait is used in passive be made to wait, not active made the passengers. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Have/get + object + V3 = arrange a service OR experience an event; context decides.
- Have/make + person + bare infinitive; get + person + to-infinitive; passive make = be made + to-infinitive.
- Linking verbs connect the subject to an adjective/noun complement; check action vs linking meaning.
- Get/go/turn/become can describe a change of state.
- Two-object verbs may use person + thing or thing + to/for + person, but patterns are verb-specific.
- Perception: bare infinitive = whole event; -ing = action in progress.
- Learn each verb with its object, preposition and complement form.
