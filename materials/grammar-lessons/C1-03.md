## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. ???????? ???? ? ??????? ???????????

Cleft structures divide a message into background information and a focused element. **It-clefts** and **what-clefts** make the information focus explicit. Inversion is a separate word-order resource: some place/direction expressions and other marked patterns move the verb before the subject. The lesson therefore distinguishes focus structures from inversion instead of treating every fronted phrase as the same construction.

The principal source units are Advanced Grammar in Use 98?100: focusing with it-/what-clauses, inversion for emphasis and other inversion patterns.

### 4.2. ???????????? ? ?????????????? ??????

**1. It-cleft: It + be + focus + that/who-clause**

- It was the delay that caused the problem.
- It was Maria who identified the error.
- It was in 2024 that the policy changed.

Use the it-clause to focus a particular element. The following that-/who-clause contains the remaining information. Choose the tense of be to match the time of the original message. Who is natural when a person is focused; that is common for other focus elements.

**2. What-cleft: What + clause + be + focus**

- What the committee objected to was the lack of transparency.
- What matters most is the quality of the evidence.
- What I need is a clear explanation.

The what-clause introduces the information being discussed; the complement after be receives focus. A that-clause can also follow the focused be-phrase: *What the review established was that the data were incomplete.*

**3. Fronted it-clause / wh-clause**

The it-/what-clause may be used to place important information at the end of the sentence, where it receives focus. Compare:
- It is not surprising that the data were incomplete.
- That the data were incomplete is not surprising.

The it-construction is often more natural when the clause would otherwise be long or heavy.

**4. Inversion for emphasis: verb before subject**

- Alex stood in the doorway. ? Alex stood in the doorway. (ordinary order)
- Rarely had I seen such a view. (negative/restrictive adverbial + auxiliary + subject)
- Here comes the bus. / There goes the last train.
- Into the room came the director.

Inversion can occur after negative or restrictive adverbials, after certain place/direction expressions, and in a number of fixed patterns. The exact trigger determines the form; fronting alone does not always cause inversion.

**5. Place/direction expressions**

With here/there and expressions such as up, down, away, back, in and out, the verb may precede a full noun subject:
- Here comes the bus.
- Away went the children.
- Down fell the leaves.

With a subject pronoun, use normal subject?verb order: **Here it comes**, not *Here comes it*.

**6. Other emphatic inversion patterns**

- So + adjective + be + subject: *So serious was the situation that...*
- Such + be + subject + that-clause: *Such was the demand that...*
- Not only + auxiliary + subject + verb: *Not only did the plan fail, but...*
- Neither/nor + auxiliary + subject: *Neither did the second test confirm it.*

Use the auxiliary appropriate to the tense and verb. With a lexical verb in the past simple, use did + subject + base verb. Do not use do-support when be or another auxiliary is already present.

**7. Inversion in conditional clauses**

- If you should need more information... ? Should you need more information...
- If I had asked, I would have been able to help. ? Had I asked, I would have been able to help.

Conditional inversion is explained in detail in C1-02; here it is included only as a cross-reference within the wider inversion system.

### 4.3. ??? ??????? ?????????? ?????

1. If the purpose is to focus one constituent, choose an it-cleft.
2. If the purpose is to make a clause the topic and focus its complement, choose a what-cleft.
3. If a long clause is the subject, consider an introductory it-construction.
4. If a negative/restrictive adverbial or a recognised emphatic pattern triggers inversion, identify the required auxiliary and invert subject and auxiliary.
5. With place/direction expressions, check whether the subject is a full noun phrase or a pronoun.
6. Do not infer inversion from fronting alone: topicalised objects usually retain normal subject?verb order.
7. For conditional inversion, use the dedicated C1-02 lesson.

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- It-clefts and what-clefts are focus structures; they are not the same as subject?auxiliary inversion.
- In a what-cleft, the whole what-clause is treated as one clause-level unit. Agreement is sensitive to the complement and construction; do not teach an absolute singular rule without checking the exact example.
- Fronting a topic or object does not automatically invert the subject and verb.
- Place/direction inversion is most natural with certain intransitive verbs and a full noun subject. A pronoun subject normally stays after the verb: *Here it comes*.
- Negative/restrictive adverbials trigger a different inversion pattern; consult C1-01 for negative inversion.
- Not only, neither/nor, so + adjective and such + be have their own auxiliary patterns. Use do-support only when required by the lexical verb and tense.
- Conditional inversion is covered in C1-02; do not mix its tense choices with other inversion patterns.
- Use marked inversion only when it improves focus, emphasis or cohesion. It is not a decorative substitute for ordinary word order.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `It was not the initial sample but the second trial where exposed the flaw.` | `It was not the initial sample but the second trial that exposed the flaw.` | Where refers to place, not the focused subject. |
| `What the committee objected to were the lack of transparency.` | `What the committee objected to was the lack of transparency.` | Were incorrectly uses plural agreement with lack. |
| `All that the review established were that the data were incomplete.` | `All that the review established was that the data were incomplete.` | Were mismatches the singular all-clause focus. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.
