## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Advanced verb complementation includes object complements, bare infinitives, -ing clauses, to-infinitives and finite that-clauses. Some verbs of perception, causation and evaluation license multiple structures with meaning differences.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced verb complementation**. Измеримая цель: Использовать сложные модели дополнений, verbs of senses и различия значения в verb patterns

### 4.2. Формирование и грамматические модели

**1. Verb + object + complement**

Some verbs take an object followed by an adjective, noun or other complement: *They found the proposal impractical; they elected her chairperson*. The complement describes or identifies the object, not the subject.

**2. Verbs with object + preposition**

Some verbs require a preposition after the object: *accuse someone of, prevent someone from, provide someone with, remind someone of*. Learn the whole pattern and the form that follows the preposition (often a noun or -ing).

**3. Verb + two objects**

Some verbs take an indirect and a direct object: *She sent me a report*. The indirect object can often be moved after the direct object with to/for: *She sent a report to me*. Some verbs allow only one of these patterns or change meaning depending on the preposition.

**4. Verbs followed by -ing**

Some verbs take an -ing clause, with or without an object: *avoid doing, consider changing, enjoy reading, prevent someone from entering*. The -ing form may describe an activity, process or event; do not substitute a to-infinitive automatically.

**5. Verbs followed by to-infinitive**

Some verbs take a to-infinitive: *agree to help, decide to leave, hope to finish*. With an object, patterns include *persuade someone to do, allow someone to do, encourage someone to do*. Learn the object requirement.

**6. Verbs of perception: bare infinitive or -ing**

- *I saw him cross the road* focuses on the event as a whole.
- *I saw him crossing the road* focuses on the activity in progress.

In passive forms, the infinitive commonly takes to: *He was seen to cross the road*. Context and verb choice matter.

**7. Verb + wh-clause / that-clause**

Some verbs take a clause introduced by what/where/how/whether or that: *I don't know what to do; she explained how the system worked; they agreed that the plan was feasible*. Whether introduces alternatives/indirect yes-no questions; it is not interchangeable with every wh-word.

**8. Complementation and meaning**

A change in complement can change meaning or grammaticality. Check whether the verb requires an object, a preposition, a particular infinitive form or a finite clause. A dictionary entry should be read for its pattern, not only its translation.

### 4.3. Как выбрать подходящую форму

1. Identify the head verb and look up its complement pattern.
2. Decide whether the verb takes an object, two objects, an object complement or a clause.
3. If a preposition is required, keep it and use the correct form after it.
4. Choose -ing or to-infinitive according to the governing verb; do not generalise from meaning alone.
5. With two objects, check whether the indirect object can move after the direct object and which preposition is required.
6. With perception verbs, choose bare infinitive for a whole event or -ing for an action in progress; check the passive form separately.
7. For wh-/that-clauses, use the correct clause marker and statement word order.
8. Re-read the sentence to ensure the complement expresses the intended meaning and does not change the participant roles.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

I saw him leave (complete event). I saw him leaving (process). He was seen to leave (passive perception). The rule prevented them from entering.

### 4.6. Исключения, ограничения и границы употребления

- Verb complementation is lexical; verbs with similar meanings may require different patterns.
- Some verbs allow both -ing and to-infinitive, sometimes with a meaning/aspect difference; others allow only one.
- Prevent requires an object + from + -ing; do not use prevent someone to do.
- Passive perception patterns commonly use to-infinitive where the active pattern uses a bare infinitive.
- Some verbs take two objects, but not every verb allows both object orders; check the required to/for pattern.
- A complement adjective/noun describes the object after verbs such as find/elect; do not use an adverb where an adjective complement is required.
- Wh-clauses and whether-clauses have their own syntax; indirect questions use statement order.
- Do not assume a verb can take a that-clause simply because its meaning seems compatible; verify its attested complement pattern.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The committee found the proposal unworkably and impractical.` | `The committee found the proposal unworkable and impractical.` | Unworkably is an adverb, not an object complement adjective. |
| `The new regulation prevents companies to sharing personal data without consent.` | `The new regulation prevents companies from sharing personal data without consent.` | To does not follow prevent in this construction. |
| `The witness was heard say that the door had been open.` | `The witness was heard to say that the door had been open.` | Bare say is used in active perception, not after passive was heard. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.

### 4.12. Краткая памятка

- Learn the verb together with its complement frame: object, two objects, object + complement, -ing, to-infinitive, preposition or clause.
- After prepositions, use the appropriate noun/-ing form.
- Perception: bare infinitive = whole event; -ing = process; passive commonly uses to-infinitive.
- Two-object patterns may allow to/for alternatives, but not universally.
- Wh-/whether/that-clauses use the correct marker and statement order.
- Complement choice can change meaning; check the full pattern, not just the dictionary translation.
