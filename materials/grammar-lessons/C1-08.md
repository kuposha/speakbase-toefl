## 4. Полный конспект

### 4.1. Основная идея и функция конструкции

Academic reporting verbs describe how a source contributes to an argument: it demonstrates, suggests, challenges, acknowledges, assumes or attributes. Verb choice signals stance, evidence strength and the relation between writer and source.

Тема этого блока — **Advanced reporting and academic citation language**. Измеримая цель: Передавать чужую позицию, различать reporting verbs, tense shifts и stance

**1. Reporting clause + reported clause**

- *She explained that she couldn't take the job until January.*
- *She explained, "I can't take the job until January."*

A reporting clause identifies the speaker/source and reporting verb; the reported clause gives the words or thought. Direct quotation preserves the original wording; indirect report adapts grammar and reference to the reporting context.

**2. Reporting statements: that-clauses and verb patterns**

Common patterns include:
- **say/claim/argue/maintain + that-clause**;
- **tell + object + that-clause**;
- **admit/deny/acknowledge + -ing or that-clause**;
- **suggest/recommend + -ing or that-clause**, but not normally *suggest someone to do*;
- **warn/advise/remind + object + (not) to-infinitive** in the appropriate meaning.

Check each reporting verb's complement. Similar meanings do not guarantee the same grammar.

**3. Negative reporting and reported questions**

- *"I didn't see the email." ? She denied seeing the email.*
- *"Where do you live?" ? He asked where I lived.*
- *"Are you coming?" ? She asked whether/if I was coming.*

Reported questions use statement word order, not question inversion. Yes/no questions use if/whether; wh-questions retain the wh-word. Do not add do-support in the reported clause.

**4. Tense choice and time reference**

Backshift is common when the reporting verb is past and the reported situation is viewed from that past point:
- present ? past; present perfect ? past perfect; will ? would.
- Past perfect normally remains past perfect.
- A general truth or a situation still true may remain in the present.
- Time/place expressions change only when the reporting context requires it (today ? that day, here ? there, etc.).

Do not apply backshift mechanically if the statement remains true or the reporting context is unchanged.

**5. Reporting offers, suggestions, orders and intentions**

- **offer/promise/agree/refuse + to-infinitive**;
- **suggest/recommend + -ing or that-clause**;
- **advise/order/warn/tell + object + to-infinitive**;
- **insist on + -ing** or **insist that + clause**, depending on meaning and variety.

The person receiving an instruction is the object in patterns such as *told them to wait*. Do not omit it where the pattern requires it.

**6. Modal verbs in reported speech**

Modal forms may change when the reporting point moves into the past: will ? would; can ? could; may ? might. Some modals (must, should, could, might, would) may remain unchanged depending on meaning. Obligation, possibility and deduction must retain their original force; do not replace every modal mechanically.

**7. Reporting with nouns and adjectives**

- *The authors made the claim that...*
- *There is an assumption that...*
- *They were confident that...*
- *The report expressed concern about...*

Reporting nouns (claim, suggestion, observation, assumption, explanation) and adjectives (confident, doubtful, certain, aware) take their own complement patterns: that-clause, wh-clause or preposition + noun/-ing.

**8. Should in that-clauses and the present subjunctive**

After verbs/adjectives expressing recommendation, demand, insistence or importance, formal English may use **that + subject + (should) + base verb** or the mandative subjunctive base form:
- *They recommended that the company should review the policy.*
- *They recommended that the company review the policy.*

Do not add -s to a third-person singular verb in the mandative subjunctive. This pattern is formal and varies by dialect/register.

**9. Academic reporting and citation stance**

Use reporting verbs and impersonal phrases to identify whether a source demonstrates, suggests, challenges, acknowledges or attributes a claim. Match the verb's strength to the evidence. *It is argued that...* and *The results are reported to...* can distance the writer from a claim; they do not verify it.

### 4.4. Сравнение с близкими конструкциями

The authors demonstrate that X (evidence strongly supports). They suggest that X (cautious interpretation). They attribute the change to Y (cause/source).

| Что проверяем | Вопрос для выбора |
|---|---|
| Форма | Какие элементы обязательны и в каком порядке они стоят? |
| Значение | Что именно меняется: время, длительность, реальность, степень уверенности или фокус? |
| Контекст | Что уже известно собеседнику и каков регистр высказывания? |
| Проверка | Если заменить форму близкой конструкцией, изменится ли смысл? Объясни, почему. |

### 4.6. ??????????, ??????????? ? ??????? ????????????

- Say normally does not take an indirect object directly (*say to someone*); tell normally requires an object when the listener is specified.
- Suggest/recommend do not normally take *object + to-infinitive*; use -ing or a that-clause.
- Reported questions use statement order; do not retain do/does/did inversion.
- Backshift is not compulsory when the reported statement remains true or the reporting context is still current.
- Time/place expressions change only when the speaker's reference point changes.
- Some modals do not backshift in the same way as will/can/may; keep the original meaning.
- Reporting verbs differ in complement pattern: deny doing, promise to do, advise someone to do, suggest doing/that..., insist on doing/that...
- Mandative should and the subjunctive base form are formal patterns; do not add third-person -s in the subjunctive.
- Reporting language expresses a source's position; it does not establish that the claim is true. Avoid upgrading suggest to prove without evidence.

### 4.7. Типичные ошибки русскоязычных студентов

| `The authors attribute the increase for changes in sampling methods.` | `The authors attribute the increase to changes in sampling methods.` | For does not complete the standard attribute + object + to pattern. |
| `The review cautions researchers from treating correlation as causation.` | `The review cautions researchers against treating correlation as causation.` | From is not the standard complement in this warning pattern. |
| `The authors argue to the existing model overlooks social factors.` | `The authors argue that the existing model overlooks social factors.` | To would require a different complement such as argue to do, not this statement. |

Русский перевод помогает понять смысл, но не управляет английской формой напрямую. Проверяй роль каждого элемента и грамматическую модель английского предложения.
